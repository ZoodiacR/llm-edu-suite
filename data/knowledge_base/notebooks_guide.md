## 00 · A complete mini LLM in one notebook
`notebooks/00_start_here/00_mini_llm_end_to_end.ipynb` · also as a script: `python scripts/mini_llm.py`

**What you build.** A whole GPT without importing anything from the project, just PyTorch: a byte-level BPE tokenizer, a modern transformer (embeddings, RoPE, RMSNorm, causal attention, SwiGLU, tied output head), the training loop (AdamW, warmup + cosine, clipping), evaluation, sampling and saving.

**Key concepts**
- How all the pieces fit together before you study them separately.
- The sanity check: the initial loss should be ≈ ln(V).
- **Irreducible loss**: since the corpus is generated by a known grammar, you can compute the theoretical minimum (4·ln 8 nats per sentence) and measure how far the model is from it.
- Why BPE stops merging once every word is already a token.

**Results.** 1.86M parameters, a 455-token vocabulary. It trains in **~22 s on GPU**. Validation loss 0.778 against a theoretical floor of 0.774: **0.6% from the optimum**. **82%** of the sampled sentences are grammatical. You can see the typical greedy-decoding loop.

**Exercises.** Train it on tiny Shakespeare (`text_path`), remove RoPE and watch what happens to the grammar, or add a KV-cache to `generate`.

## 01 · Tokenization
`notebooks/01_architecture/01_tokenization.ipynb` · module `tokenizer`

**What you build.** A byte-level BPE with training, encoding, decoding, special tokens and save/load.

**Key concepts.** Why subwords are used; why byte-level BPE never produces `<UNK>`; how vocabulary size trades sequence length against the size of the embedding table; **fertility** (tokens per word) as a measure of how well a tokenizer covers a domain or language.

**Results.** The example sentence takes 52 bytes and is encoded into 7 tokens (7.43 bytes/token), with an exact round trip. Fertility is **1.00 on the training domain versus 3.90 outside it** (code and Spanish): this is why under-represented languages cost more tokens.

<a id="02"></a>
## 02 · Attention, RoPE and normalization
`notebooks/01_architecture/02_attention_rope_norm.ipynb` · modules `layers`, `model`

**What you build.** RMSNorm (checked against `torch.nn.RMSNorm`), RoPE with Llama 3.1-style scaling, hand-written attention (checked against `F.scaled_dot_product_attention`), MHA → GQA → MQA → MLA, and the KV-cache.

**Key concepts.** RoPE depends only on relative distance (the notebook verifies it numerically). GQA shares K/V heads to shrink the cache. MLA (DeepSeek) compresses K/V into a latent vector. Incremental generation with the cache must give exactly the same result as the full forward pass.

**Results.** Training the same network with each variant: **MHA uses 2048 B/token of cache versus 160 B/token for MLA, with the same perplexity (2.65)**. GQA-2 and MQA sit in between (512 and 256 B/token). In DeepSeek-V3 the per-layer reduction is about 57×.

<a id="03"></a>
## 03 · Efficient attention for long contexts
`notebooks/01_architecture/03_efficient_attention.ipynb` · module `layers`

**What you build.** Sliding-window attention (SWA), Longformer, FlexAttention (if your PyTorch ships it), content-selected block-sparse attention, linear attention (with its constant-state recurrent form) and hybrids.

**Key concepts.** O(T²) versus O(T·w) versus O(T) cost; the receptive field of stacked SWA; why linear attention has a fixed-size state; **picking the right metric**: global perplexity is useless for this task, copy accuracy is not.

**Results.** Copy task at a distance of ~42 tokens, 1500 steps:

| Variant | Copy accuracy |
|---|---|
| dense (GQA) | **99.9%** |
| block-sparse | **88.7%** |
| hybrid (1 global layer in every 4) | 48.4% |
| sliding window w=16 | 0.6% |
| linear | 0.3% |

With only 300 steps even the dense model sits at 0.3%: ***induction heads*** appear abruptly during training (a phase transition).

<a id="04"></a>
## 04 · FFN and Mixture of Experts
`notebooks/01_architecture/04_ffn_moe.ipynb` · module `layers`

**What you build.** SwiGLU with Llama's sizing rule, an MoE layer with top-k routing, shared experts, auxiliary-loss balancing and *aux-free* balancing (DeepSeek-V3).

**Key concepts.** Total versus active parameters; why the router collapses without balancing; why the aux-free bias update must be small; a fair comparison at equal active compute.

**Results.** With top-2 out of 8 experts, the model has 3× more total parameters than active ones. Aux-free balancing takes an unbalanced router from **4.0× to 1.15×** with `bias_lr=0.01`, while with 0.1 it oscillates (visible in the plot). On the toy corpus the MoE does not beat the dense model (ppl 3.31 versus 3.24); the notebook explains why MoE pays off at scale.

## 05 · Pre-training
`notebooks/02_pretraining_and_inference/05_pretraining.ipynb` · modules `training`, `data`

**What you build.** The full pipeline: text → tokenizer → model → a loop with AdamW, warmup + cosine, clipping, checkpoints and a dashboard (loss, gradient norm, LR); then generation, saving and loading.

**Key concepts.** Sanity checks (initial loss ≈ ln V, a test that the model cannot see the future); how to read the dashboard; **irreducible loss** (Chinchilla's *E* term); a schedule ablation (cosine versus WSD); a scaling sanity check.

**Results.** Validation perplexity 2.79 against a theoretical floor of 2.75 (**1.5% above the floor** in loss). At the same number of steps, the 1.67M model learns much faster than the 125K one (loss 1.054 versus 1.259 at step 50). Cosine and WSD end up practically identical.

<a id="06"></a>
## 06 · Inference and serving
`notebooks/02_pretraining_and_inference/06_inference_serving.ipynb` · modules `inference`, `model`

**What you build.** Sampling strategies, the KV-cache, **speculative decoding from scratch** (with a check that it preserves the distribution), batching, and TTFT and throughput measurement.

**Key concepts.** Temperature, top-k and top-p as transformations of the distribution (plot and entropy); diversity (distinct-2); why speculative decoding is exact; decoding is memory-bound; and the GPU kernel-launch *overhead* with small models.

**Results.** At the start of a word, top-k 20 cuts the entropy from 4.25 to 2.89 nats. Greedy produces the same continuation 16 times out of 16. Speculative decoding **accepts 98% of the draft tokens, ~4.6 tokens per pass of the large model**. Batch 16 delivers ~17× the tokens/s of batch 1. On GPU, with a model this small, the KV-cache barely speeds things up (the notebook explains why and when that changes).

<a id="07"></a>
## 07 · Fine-tuning I: SFT, LoRA and distillation
`notebooks/03_post_training/07_finetuning_sft_lora.ipynb` · module `finetune`

**What you build.** SFT with *loss masking* (loss on the response only), LoRA from scratch (identical to the base at initialization, and mergeable), a rank ablation, and teacher–student distillation.

**Key concepts.**
- Chat templates and `-100` labels.
- **Why LoRA can fail**: the new role tokens have frozen embeddings. The fix is to train the embeddings too, the equivalent of `modules_to_save` in peft.
- Rank and capacity.
- Sequence-level distillation (the teacher writes data) versus logit distillation.

**Results**

| Method | Exact match |
|---|---|
| base | 0% |
| full SFT | **100%** |
| LoRA, adapters only | 0% (failure explained) |
| LoRA + embeddings (16.5% of the parameters) | **95.3%** |
| rank ablation r = 1 / 4 / 16 (800 steps) | 1.3% / 1.3% / 48.0% |
| student with 1k human labels | 24.7% |
| student + 6k teacher responses | **98.0%** |
| student + logit distillation | 44.7%: fragile, the notebook explains why |

<a id="08"></a>
## 08 · Fine-tuning II: DPO and GRPO
`notebooks/03_post_training/08_preference_rl_dpo_grpo.ipynb` · module `finetune`

**What you build.** DPO with its diagnostics (initial loss ln 2, preference accuracy, margin, log-probs), DPO + NLL, and GRPO with verifiable rewards (exact versus dense).

**Key concepts.**
- ***Likelihood displacement***: DPO can also lower the probability of the good response.
- The NLL anchor (`rpo_alpha` in trl).
- Group-relative advantage in GRPO.
- Reward design and **Goodhart's law**: the proxy reward is not the metric.
- Why the value of the policy-gradient loss is ≈ 0 even though its gradient is not.

**Results** (starting from a partial SFT at 70.7%):

| Method | Exact match | Perplexity |
|---|---|---|
| SFT | 70.7% | 1.76 |
| + DPO | 46.7% | 3.66 |
| **+ DPO + NLL** | **80.0%** | **1.46** |
| + GRPO (exact reward) | 74.0% | 1.61 |
| + GRPO (dense reward) | 72.7% | 1.59 |

## 09 · Quantization
`notebooks/02_pretraining_and_inference/09_quantization.ipynb` · module `quant`

**What you build.** Per-tensor, per-row and block-wise absmax quantization; NF4 (QLoRA); `QuantizedLinear`; full-model quantization.

**Key concepts.** Why finer scales reduce error; NF4 places its levels where the weights are (normal distribution); **the metrics that actually detect degradation**: KL against the fp model and top-1 agreement, which is what llama.cpp uses. Perplexity alone is not very sensitive.

**Results.** At 4 bits the relative weight error drops from 96.7% (per tensor) to 9.6% (blocks of 32). On the full model: INT8 has 99.9% top-1 agreement; **NF4 98.6% at 0.31× the memory**; 3 bits 94.8%; and 2 bits 87.9%, with a KL 100 times larger, while perplexity rises by only 1.2%.

## 10 · RAG
`notebooks/04_applications/10_rag.ipynb` · modules `rag`, `evaluation`

**What you build.** Chunking, embeddings, BM25, hybrid search with *Reciprocal Rank Fusion*, prompts with citations, query expansion and the full evaluation.

**Key concepts.** Dense versus lexical search and why you combine them; retrieval metrics (recall@k, MRR, nDCG) kept separate from generation metrics (token-F1, *faithfulness*); a *pairwise* LLM judge.

**Results.** Dense recall@1 0.88; BM25 and **hybrid 1.00**. Answer token-F1 0.70, with context support 1.00. In the RAG versus no-RAG comparison, the judge prefers RAG in 7 out of 8 cases.

## 12 · Recommender systems
`notebooks/04_applications/12_recommender_systems.ipynb` · module `recsys`

**What you build.** Sequential recommendation as "next-item prediction" (SASRec-style), collaborative and content embeddings, and re-ranking with an LLM.

**Key concepts.** *Leave-one-out* evaluation; baselines (random, popularity, content); cold start; an LLM re-ranker is only worth it if it improves the offline metrics.

**Results.** hit@10: random 0.032, popularity 0.097, content 0.059 and **SASRec 0.210**. Re-ranking with the MockLLM **makes things worse** (0.187 → 0.153). It is an honest result, and the notebook explains what to learn from it.

## 13 · Modern LLM applications
`notebooks/04_applications/13_modern_llm_applications.ipynb` · module `applications`

**What you build.** Structured extraction, zero/few-shot classification, map-reduce summarization, a semantic cache, a *prompt injection* guard, batch processing with cost tracking, and synthetic data generation with filtering.

**Key concepts.** Per-field accuracy; the confusion matrix; ROUGE versus *faithfulness*; the cache threshold trades hits for wrong answers; defense in depth against injection; cost projection.

**Results.** Extraction 3/3 on every field. Classification 100%. Semantic cache with a 50% hit rate and 1 false hit on 2 near-identical pairs. Guard: catches 4/4 attacks with 0/4 false positives. Projected cost of $43 per million records. Synthetic data: 82% kept after filtering.

## 14 · Agentic AI
`notebooks/05_agents_and_mcp/14_agentic_ai.ipynb` · module `agentic`

**What you build.** Budgets and loop detection, plan-and-execute, verifier-guided reflection, short-term (summary) and long-term (vector) memory, a multi-agent supervisor, and trajectory evaluation.

**Key concepts.** Workflow versus agent; every agent needs a **stop reason**; evaluate the *path* (the right tools, in order and without waste), not just the answer; reflection needs a reliable verifier; memory is retrieval, and its recall has to be measured; routing is classification.

**Results**
- Plan-and-execute solves **6/6 multi-step tasks versus 2/6** for a single loop (with the MockLLM; the notebook explains the caveat).
- All 4 stop policies fire correctly.
- Short-term memory stays at ~160 tokens versus 640 for the full history.
- Long-term memory recall@1 is 4/6, with a lexical embedder.
- Routing: 67% (embeddings) versus 42% (LLM).

## 15 · Observability and LLMOps
`notebooks/06_llmops/15_observability_llmops.ipynb` · module `observability`

**What you build.** Traces and spans with OpenTelemetry semantics, a wrapper for LLMs and tools, PII redaction, percentile metrics, SLOs, per-trace quality scores, and export (JSONL and OTLP).

**Key concepts.** Trace, span, tree; why p95/p99 and not the mean; retries visible in the trace; *online* quality (groundedness, correctness); privacy; sampling.

**Results.** 120 simulated requests with long-tail latency and 4% failures. Per request: **p50 45 ms, p95 130 ms, p99 171 ms**, 1% errors after retries, ~$0.00014 per request. The SLOs are met. Mean token-F1 0.88, and drill-down to the worst trace.

## 16 · MCP: Model Context Protocol
`notebooks/05_agents_and_mcp/16_mcp_model_context_protocol.ipynb` · module `mcp`, script `scripts/mcp_server.py`

**What you build.** An MCP server and client from scratch, the JSON-RPC messages seen "on the wire", tools, resources and prompts, an agent that uses MCP tools, a real stdio server, and security controls.

**Key concepts.** Host, client and server; the *handshake* (`initialize`); requests versus notifications; protocol errors versus `isError`; stdio (never write to stdout); allowlists, *namespacing*, confirmation of destructive actions, injection in tool results, and *tool poisoning*.

**Results.** **7/7 conformance checks** over real stdio. Search finds the right document in 6/6 questions. A stdio round trip takes ~0.2 ms (negligible next to an LLM call). The injection hidden in a tool result is detected and isolated.
